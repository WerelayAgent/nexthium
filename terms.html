2. What Nexthium does

Nexthium is software that AI clients and other programs call to:

  • read public data from Robinhood Chain and its block explorers;
  • decode transactions, run them in simulation and describe them in plain words;
  • build unsigned transactions and hand them to a wallet you control for approval;
  • broadcast transactions you have signed yourself;
  • write smart contract source from templates, compile it and send it for explorer verification; and
  • read from certain protocols run by others and build transactions for them.

Nexthium serves Robinhood Chain mainnet (chain ID 4663) and the Robinhood Chain development network (chain ID 46630). Every deployment has one default chain, and any request can name the other.

3. Who can use it

  • The laws that apply to you allow you to use the Service.
  • You meet the sanctions terms in section 16.

It is up to you to check that using the Service, and each protocol you reach through it, is lawful where you live.

4. Your keys and assets

The points below describe how the Service is built, and every other section rests on them.

  • Your assets stay with you. Nexthium keeps no wallet on your behalf and takes custody of no digital asset.
  • Your keys stay with you. The Service asks for no private key or seed phrase, receives none and stores none. Keep them to yourself, including from anyone who says they speak for Nexthium.
  • You do the signing. Each transaction is signed in a wallet that only you control, or by software that you run.
  • Trades happen between you and the protocol. A swap or deposit built by the Service is a transaction from your wallet straight to the contracts of a protocol run by others.

Using the Service creates no broker, dealer, exchange, custodian, money transmitter, investment adviser or fiduciary relationship between you and Nexthium.

5. What you look after

The choice to sign is yours alone, and so is responsibility for each transaction your keys sign. That covers:

  • reading every transaction before you sign it: recipient, amount, chain, explanation and risk flags;
  • checking what your own wallet displays, since the wallet has the final word on what gets signed;
  • keeping your wallet, devices, private keys and agent keys secure;
  • the addresses and amounts that you or your client provide;
  • each contract you generate, deploy or call, including reading its source; and
  • the taxes and reporting that your transactions bring with them.

Which chain a request uses

This deployment builds transactions for mainnet unless a request names the other chain. A mainnet transaction moves real assets once you sign it, and the development network is there for trying a new flow first.

6. Assistants and autonomous agents

You reach the Service through an AI client or other software run by someone other than us. That software chooses which tools to call and how to put the results to you.

  • The approval page shows the simulation and explanation that Nexthium produced, and your wallet shows what you are about to sign. Those two are the record of a transaction; an assistant's summary of it is a retelling.
  • Each AI client and its provider answer for their own conduct, output and handling of data.
  • When you run an autonomous agent that signs with its own key, you answer for what it signs, the funds you give it and the limits you set for it. Spending limits and allow lists for such an agent are yours to set up.

7. Information Nexthium returns

The Service returns information that software derives from public data. It is general information for your own decisions, and it carries no personal recommendation to buy, sell or hold an asset or to use a protocol.

Simulations

A simulation runs the transaction against one recent block and reports what happened there.

Risk flags

Risk flags come from a fixed set of structural rules, each listed on the security page. They describe what those rules see in the transaction.

Contract templates

Templates build on open source libraries, OpenZeppelin Contracts in particular, and give you a starting point. Read and test the source before you deploy it, and arrange an independent review when the value involved calls for one.

Market data

Prices, quotes, funding rates and similar figures come from on-chain sources and from the protocols that publish them.

8. Protocols run by others

The Service reads from, and builds transactions for, protocols and services that other companies operate. Today these are Uniswap for swaps, Lighter for perpetual futures, 0xbow Privacy Pools for private payments, Blockscout for explorer data, and RPC node providers.

  • Each of them runs independently of Nexthium, and Nexthium is no party to a transaction between you and them.
  • Their own terms and eligibility rules apply to your use of them, and it is up to you to read and follow those.
  • The Service builds deposits into Lighter; positions are opened and managed on Lighter itself.
  • Lighter says it serves no one in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Switzerland, the United Arab Emirates or Singapore. Use the perps tools only from a place Lighter serves.
  • Rules for privacy protocols differ from place to place. For Privacy Pools the Service reads public pool data only, and deposit secrets stay with 0xbow's app in your browser.

9. Agent keys

  • An agent key is optional. You create one by signing a message that proves you control a wallet, and each wallet may hold five active keys at a time.
  • We show a key one time. Nexthium keeps only a hash of it, so a lost key is replaced by creating a new one.
  • Keep your keys private; all activity under a key is yours. Revoke a key straight away if you think someone else has seen it.
  • Keys are for you and for agents you run. Selling or publishing them is outside what they are for.
  • A published agent profile must be accurate, lawful and respectful of other people's rights. Profiles that fall short of that are taken out of the directory.

11. Call limits

Calls are counted per minute. Open access allows 120 calls, 10 of them heavy, and an agent key allows 600 calls, 60 of them heavy. A call over the limit gets a rate limit reply and can be sent again in the next minute. Limits may be updated, and this page always shows the current figures.

12. Fees and gas

Reads, simulations, ordinary prepared transactions and agent keys carry no charge from Nexthium.

Swap fee

Swaps built through the Service may carry a Nexthium fee. When one applies:

  • it is a share of the swap output, paid in the token you receive, with a ceiling of 1% set in the Nexthium server that builds the transaction;
  • it is collected within the swap transaction itself, so it is paid only when the swap completes;
  • the rate, the fee amount and what you receive after it appear in the quote, in the preview and on the approval page before you sign.

Nexthium adds nothing to the price itself, which comes from the protocol.

Gas on each network is paid in ETH by you, as are any swap, trading, funding or other fees a protocol charges. Those amounts are set by the networks and protocols, and the Service shows an estimate wherever it can make one.

Any paid plan will be published with its terms before it starts, and applies to you only if you choose it.
